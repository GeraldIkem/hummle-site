Open it. Press Play.

That’s the whole job. Hummle reads the page, works out how each line should sound, and starts speaking within seconds, all on your own computer.

three steps, and the third is optional

  1. Bring something to read

    On a chapter, article or blog in Chrome or Edge, click Hummle. It pulls out the text and shows the first and last lines, so you can check nothing was missed. Menus, ads and comments are left behind.

    Writing your own? Paste it into the Studio. Only want a paragraph? Select it and choose Read selection.

  2. Listen and follow along

    The sentence being read lights up. Click any line to jump there, change the speed, set a sleep timer. Hummle remembers where you stopped.

    System messages get their own chime, effects land on the words they belong to, and author’s notes are skipped or read, as you prefer.

  3. Make it yours, if you want to

    Pick a voice. Choose how much feeling you want: Subtle, Natural or Dramatic. If a line comes out wrong, click it and choose “less”, “no sound here” or a different emotion. Hummle remembers your fix for the rest of the book.

What happens when you press Play

Four things, in about the time it takes to read this sentence.

  1. Reads the sceneWho is speaking, to whom, and what just happened.
  2. Plans the deliveryPace, pauses, emotion and any sounds, without changing a word.
  3. SpeaksIn the voice you chose, a few sentences ahead of you.
  4. Checks itselfListens back and redoes any line with a missed or extra word.
